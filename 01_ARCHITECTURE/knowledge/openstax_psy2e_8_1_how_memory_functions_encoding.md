## 2. Concepte Extrase și Validate Verbatim

### 1. Encoding
Encoding is the initial process of inputting information into the memory system. During this process, the brain labels, codes, and organizes sensory information by connecting new concepts to existing ones.

> "We get information into our brains through a process called encoding , which is the input of information into the memory system."

### 2. Automatic Processing
Automatic processing involves the encoding of details such as time, space, frequency, and word meaning without conscious awareness. It typically occurs for familiar information or routine tasks.

> "This is known as automatic processing , or the encoding of details like time, space, frequency, and the meaning of words."

### 3. Effortful Processing
Effortful processing requires conscious work and attention to encode information. This is typically necessary when learning new, complex material or skills.

> "But what about the actual test material you studied? It probably required a lot of work and attention on your part in order to encode that information."

### 4. Semantic Encoding
Semantic encoding is the processing and encoding of words and their meanings. Research indicates that this method leads to better memory retention compared to visual or acoustic encoding because it involves a deeper level of processing.

> "The encoding of words and their meaning is known as semantic encoding ."

### 5. Visual Encoding
Visual encoding is the process of encoding images. Concrete, high-imagery words are easier to remember than abstract words because they can be encoded both visually and semantically.

> "Visual encoding is the encoding of images, and acoustic encoding is the encoding of sounds, words in particular."

### 6. Acoustic Encoding
Acoustic encoding is the process of encoding the sounds that words make. This method is frequently utilized in education through the use of song, rhyme, and rhythm to improve memory.

> "We encode the sounds the words make."

### 7. Self-Reference Effect
The self-reference effect is the psychological tendency to better remember information that relates to oneself. It is considered a highly effective strategy for processing verbal information.

> "The self-reference effect is the tendency for an individual to have better memory for information that relates to oneself in comparison to material that has less personal relevance"

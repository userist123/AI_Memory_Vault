## 2. Concepte Extrase și Validate Verbatim

### 1. Levels of Processing Theory
Proposed by Fergus Craik and Robert Lockhart, this theory suggests that information processed more deeply is more likely to be stored in long-term memory. It emphasizes linking new information to existing memories to increase meaningfulness.

> "In a famous article, Fergus Craik and Robert Lockhart (1972) discussed their belief that information we process more deeply goes into long-term memory. Their theory is called levels of processing ."

### 2. Self-Reference Effect
This technique involves making study material personally meaningful to the learner. By relating concepts to one's own life or existing knowledge, the learner builds a web of retrieval cues that facilitates memory access.

> "As you go through the process of elaborative rehearsal, it would be even more beneficial to make the material you are trying to memorize personally meaningful to you. In other words, make use of the self-reference effect."

### 3. Distributed Practice
This strategy involves studying over time in short durations rather than cramming. It allows sufficient time for memory consolidation and prevents the cognitive interference that can occur when links between concepts become over-activated.

> "Study across time in short durations rather than trying to cram it all in at once. Memory consolidation takes time, and studying across time allows time for memories to consolidate."

### 4. Neurogenesis
Neurogenesis is the growth of new brain cells in the hippocampus, a brain region critical for memory and learning. Research indicates that regular aerobic exercise promotes this process, thereby benefiting memory.

> "Aerobic exercise promotes neurogenesis: the growth of new brain cells in the hippocampus, an area of the brain known to play a role in memory and learning."

### 5. Mnemonic Devices
Mnemonic devices are memory aids used to assist in the recall of information. Common types include acronyms, acrostics, and jingles, which organize information into patterns or phrases that are easier to remember.

> "As you learned earlier in this chapter, mnemonic devices often help us to remember and recall information."

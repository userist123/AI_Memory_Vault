## 2. Concepte Extrase și Dovezi Textuale Verificate

### 1. Anterograde Amnesia
Anterograde amnesia is a condition where a person cannot remember new information following brain trauma, despite retaining memories of events prior to the injury, often linked to hippocampus damage and an inability to consolidate memories.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "With anterograde amnesia , you cannot remember new information, although you can remember information and events that happened prior to your injury. The hippocampus is usually affected"

- **Termeni cheie**: anterograde amnesia, hippocampus, consolidate memories

### 2. Retrograde Amnesia
Retrograde amnesia involves the loss of memory for events occurring prior to trauma, causing individuals to have difficulty accessing some or all past episodic memories.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Retrograde amnesia is loss of memory for events that occurred prior to the trauma. People with retrograde amnesia cannot remember some or even all of their past. They have difficulty remembering episodic memories."

- **Termeni cheie**: retrograde amnesia, episodic memories, trauma

### 3. Memory Construction and Reconstruction
Formulating new memories is referred to as construction, whereas retrieving prior memories is reconstruction, during which memories become flexible in short-term storage and can be modified or distorted.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "The formulation of new memories is sometimes called construction , and the process of bringing up old memories is called reconstruction . Yet as we retrieve our memories, we also tend to alter and modify them."

- **Termeni cheie**: construction, reconstruction, retrieval

### 4. Suggestibility and False Memories
Suggestibility refers to the influence of external misinformation that results in the creation of false memories, making eyewitness accounts vulnerable to modification by outside cues or news reports.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Suggestibility describes the effects of misinformation from external sources that leads to the creation of false memories."

- **Termeni cheie**: suggestibility, misinformation, false memories

### 5. Eyewitness Identification Fallibility
Eyewitness testimony can be compromised by suggestive police identification procedures such as biased lineups or reinforcing feedback, potentially leading to wrongful convictions.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Gary Wells and Deah Quinlivan (2009) assert it’s suggestive police identification procedures, such as stacking lineups to make the defendant stand out, telling the witness which person to identify, and confirming witnesses choices by telling them “Good choice,” or “You picked the guy.”"

- **Termeni cheie**: eyewitness identification, lineups, wrongful convictions

### 6. Misinformation Effect Paradigm
Developed by Elizabeth Loftus, the misinformation effect paradigm demonstrates that exposure to additional, potentially inaccurate information after an event can cause an individual to misremember the original occurrence.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Loftus also developed the misinformation effect paradigm , which holds that after exposure to additional and possibly inaccurate information, a person may misremember the original event."

- **Termeni cheie**: misinformation effect paradigm, Elizabeth Loftus, misremember

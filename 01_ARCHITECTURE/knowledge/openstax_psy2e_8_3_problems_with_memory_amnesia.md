## 2. Concepte Extrase și Validate Verbatim

### 1. Amnesia
Amnesia is defined as the loss of long-term memory. It is typically caused by physical trauma, psychological trauma, or disease.

> "Amnesia is the loss of long-term memory that occurs as the result of disease, physical trauma, or psychological trauma."

### 2. Anterograde Amnesia
Anterograde amnesia is a condition where an individual cannot form new memories following a brain injury, though they retain memories from before the trauma. It is often associated with damage to the hippocampus and an inability to consolidate information from short-term to long-term memory.

> "Anterograde amnesia is commonly caused by brain trauma, such as a blow to the head. With anterograde amnesia , you cannot remember new information, although you can remember information and events that happened prior to your injury."

### 3. Retrograde Amnesia
Retrograde amnesia involves the loss of memory for events that occurred before the onset of trauma. Individuals with this condition may struggle to recall some or all of their past life experiences.

> "Retrograde amnesia is loss of memory for events that occurred prior to the trauma. People with retrograde amnesia cannot remember some or even all of their past."

### 4. Procedural Memory in Amnesia
Research indicates that individuals suffering from anterograde amnesia may still be capable of forming new procedural memories despite being unable to form new episodic or semantic memories. This is evidenced by improvements in task performance over time, even without conscious recollection of the task.

> "Many people with this form of amnesia are unable to form new episodic or semantic memories, but are still able to form new procedural memories"

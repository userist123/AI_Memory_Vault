# 📖 Funcțiile Memoriei: Codificare și Niveluri de Procesare (OpenStax)

## 2. Concepte Extrase și Dovezi Textuale Verificate

### 1. Memory Encoding
Encoding is the process of taking sensory information received from the environment and inputting, labeling, or coding it into the memory system.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "We get information into our brains through a process called encoding , which is the input of information into the memory system. Once we receive sensory information from the environment, our brains label or code it."

- **Termeni cheie**: encoding, memory system, sensory information

### 2. Automatic vs. Effortful Processing
Information encoding occurs either without conscious awareness through automatic processing of details like time and space, or through effortful processing which requires work and focused attention.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "This is known as automatic processing , or the encoding of details like time, space, frequency, and the meaning of words. Automatic processing is usually done without any conscious awareness."

- **Termeni cheie**: automatic processing, effortful processing, conscious awareness

### 3. Semantic Encoding
Semantic encoding refers to the processing and encoding of words and their meanings, which was first demonstrated experimentally by William Bousfield in 1935.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "The encoding of words and their meaning is known as semantic encoding . It was first demonstrated by William Bousfield (1935) in an experiment in which he asked people to memorize words."

- **Termeni cheie**: semantic encoding, William Bousfield, meaning

### 4. Visual Encoding and High-Imagery Words
Visual encoding involves the encoding of images; concrete, high-imagery words are more easily remembered because they are encoded both visually and semantically.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Visual encoding is the encoding of images, and acoustic encoding is the encoding of sounds, words in particular."

- **Termeni cheie**: visual encoding, images, acoustic encoding

### 5. High-Imagery Dual Coding
Concrete words that produce mental pictures are encoded both visually and semantically, which constructs a stronger memory trace than abstract, low-imagery words.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "High-imagery words are encoded both visually and semantically (Paivio, 1986), thus building a stronger memory."

- **Termeni cheie**: high-imagery words, visual encoding, semantic encoding

### 6. Acoustic Encoding
Acoustic encoding is the process of encoding the sounds that words make, which facilitates memorization through songs, rhymes, and rhythms.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "These lessons are easy to remember because of acoustic encoding. We encode the sounds the words make. This is one of the reasons why much of what we teach young children is done through song, rhyme, and rhythm."

- **Termeni cheie**: acoustic encoding, rhyme, sounds

### 7. Levels of Processing in Encoding
Craik and Tulving demonstrated that words encoded semantically are remembered better than those encoded visually or acoustically because semantic encoding involves deeper processing.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Words that had been encoded semantically were better remembered than those encoded visually or acoustically. Semantic encoding involves a deeper level of processing than the shallower visual or acoustic encoding."

- **Termeni cheie**: levels of processing, semantic encoding, Craik and Tulving

### 8. Self-Reference Effect
The self-reference effect is the cognitive tendency for individuals to better remember information that has personal relevance or relates directly to themselves.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "The self-reference effect is the tendency for an individual to have better memory for information that relates to oneself in comparison to material that has less personal relevance (Rogers, Kuiper, & Kirker, 1977)."

- **Termeni cheie**: self-reference effect, personal relevance, memory

## 2. Concepte Extrase și Validate Verbatim

### 1. Engram
The engram is defined as the group of neurons that serve as the physical representation of memory. Karl Lashley conducted early research attempting to locate this physical trace by creating lesions in the brains of rats.

> "the group of neurons that serve as the “physical representation of memory”"

### 2. Equipotentiality Hypothesis
Proposed by Karl Lashley, this hypothesis suggests that if a specific part of the brain involved in memory is damaged, other parts of that same area can compensate and take over the memory function. This was formulated after Lashley observed that rats could still navigate mazes despite brain lesions.

> "if part of one area of the brain involved in memory is damaged, another part of the same area can take over that memory function"

### 3. Amygdala
The amygdala is a part of the brain specifically involved in the processing of fear and the formation of fear-related memories.

> "The amygdala is involved in fear and fear memories."

### 4. Hippocampus
The hippocampus is associated with the processing of declarative, episodic, and recognition memory. It is one of the primary brain structures identified as being involved in memory functions.

> "The hippocampus is associated with declarative and episodic memory as well as recognition memory."

### 5. Cerebellum
The cerebellum is responsible for processing procedural memories. An example of this function is the ability to remember how to perform physical tasks, such as playing the piano.

> "The cerebellum plays a role in processing procedural memories, such as how to play the piano."

### 6. Prefrontal Cortex
The prefrontal cortex is identified as a key part of the brain involved in memory. It appears to play a specific role in the process of remembering semantic tasks.

> "The prefrontal cortex appears to be involved in remembering semantic tasks."

## 2. Concepte Extrase și Validate Verbatim

### 1. Memory Construction and Reconstruction
Memory construction refers to the initial formation of new memories, while reconstruction is the process of retrieving old memories. During retrieval, memories are flexible and can be altered or modified, often leading to inaccuracies and distortions when combined with new information.

> "The formulation of new memories is sometimes called construction , and the process of bringing up old memories is called reconstruction . Yet as we retrieve our memories, we also tend to alter and modify them."

### 2. Suggestibility
Suggestibility is a phenomenon where misinformation from external sources influences an individual's memory, potentially leading to the creation of false memories. This vulnerability can cause people to claim they remember events that were actually only suggestions made by others.

> "Suggestibility describes the effects of misinformation from external sources that leads to the creation of false memories."

### 3. Misinformation Effect
The misinformation effect paradigm, developed by Elizabeth Loftus, posits that exposure to inaccurate information after an event can cause a person to misremember the original event. Research shows that leading questions or specific word choices can significantly alter a witness's memory of details.

> "Loftus also developed the misinformation effect paradigm , which holds that after exposure to additional and possibly inaccurate information, a person may misremember the original event."

### 4. False Memory Syndrome
False memory syndrome refers to the recall of false autobiographical memories of events that did not actually occur. This concept is central to the controversy surrounding repressed and recovered memories, particularly in cases of alleged childhood trauma.

> "Recall of false autobiographical memories is called false memory syndrome ."

### 5. Eyewitness Misidentification
Eyewitness misidentification is a leading cause of wrongful convictions, as memory is fragile and susceptible to suggestive police procedures. Unintended cues from investigators, such as confirming a witness's choice or using biased lineups, can increase a witness's false confidence in their identification.

> "In studying cases where DNA evidence has exonerated people from crimes, the Innocence Project discovered that eyewitness misidentification is a leading cause of wrongful convictions"

## 2. Concepte Extrase și Validate Verbatim

### 1. Confidence Interval
A confidence interval is an estimate for an unknown population mean, constructed using a point estimate and a margin of error. It provides a range of values within which the true population parameter is expected to fall, based on a chosen confidence level.

> "The confidence interval estimate will have the form: (point estimate - error bound, point estimate + error bound)"

### 2. Error Bound for a Population Mean (EBM)
The EBM, or margin of error, represents the distance from the sample mean to the endpoints of the confidence interval. It is calculated based on the confidence level, the population standard deviation, and the sample size.

> "Here, the margin of error ( EBM ) is called the error bound for a population mean (abbreviated EBM )."

### 3. Confidence Level (CL)
The confidence level represents the percentage of confidence intervals that would contain the true population parameter if repeated samples were taken. It is mathematically related to alpha, where the sum of the confidence level and alpha equals one.

> "the confidence level is the percent of confidence intervals that contain the true population parameter when repeated samples are taken."

### 4. Standard Error of the Mean
The standard error of the mean is the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample means. It is calculated as the population standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size.

> "The fraction σ n σ n , is commonly called the "standard error of the mean" in order to distinguish clearly the standard deviation for a mean from the population standard deviation σ ."

### 5. Effect of Sample Size on Confidence Intervals
Increasing the sample size reduces the error bound, resulting in a narrower confidence interval. Conversely, decreasing the sample size increases the error bound, leading to a wider confidence interval.

> "Increasing the sample size causes the error bound to decrease, making the confidence interval narrower. Decreasing the sample size causes the error bound to increase, making the confidence interval wider."

### 6. Calculating Required Sample Size
Researchers can determine the necessary sample size for a study by rearranging the error bound formula. When calculating the required sample size, the result should always be rounded up to the next higher integer to ensure the margin of error is maintained.

> "Always round the answer UP to the next higher integer to ensure that the sample size is large enough."

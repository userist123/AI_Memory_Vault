## 2. Concepte Extrase și Dovezi Textuale Verificate

### 1. Chunking
Chunking is a memory strategy where incoming information is organized into manageable bits or chunks, commonly used for memorizing dates and phone numbers.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Another strategy is chunking : you organize information into manageable bits or chunks (Bodie, Powers, & Fitch-Hauser, 2006). Chunking is useful when trying to remember information like dates and phone numbers."

- **Termeni cheie**: chunking, manageable bits, phone numbers

### 2. Elaborative Rehearsal
Elaborative rehearsal enhances memory retention by focusing on the meaning of new information and connecting it to existing knowledge stored in long-term memory.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "You could also enhance memory by using elaborative rehearsal : a technique in which you think about the meaning of new information and its relation to knowledge already stored in your memory (Tigner, 1999)."

- **Termeni cheie**: elaborative rehearsal, meaning, long-term memory

### 3. Mnemonic Devices
Mnemonic devices serve as cognitive aids that organize information for encoding and are particularly effective for learning multi-step sequences, stages, or systems.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Mnemonic devices are memory aids that help us organize information for encoding ( Figure 8.18 ). They are especially useful when we want to recall larger bits of information such as steps, stages, phases, and parts of a system (Bellezza, 1981)."

- **Termeni cheie**: mnemonic devices, encoding, memory aids

### 4. Acronyms and Acrostics
Acronyms form a pronounceable word using the first letters of items to be remembered, while acrostics create memorable sentences from those initial letters to remember sequences like the mathematical order of operations.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "An acronym is a word formed by the first letter of each of the words you want to remember. For example, even if you live near one, you might have difficulty recalling the names of all five Great Lakes. What if I told you to think of the word Homes? HOMES is an acronym that represents Huron, Ontario, Michigan, Erie, and Superior: the five Great Lakes. Another type of mnemonic device is an acrostic: you make a phrase of all the first letters of the words."

- **Termeni cheie**: acronym, acrostic, HOMES, order of operations

### 5. Expressive Writing and Short-Term Memory
Engaging in expressive writing specifically about traumatic personal experiences can expand short-term memory capacity over several weeks.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Masao Yogo and Shuji Fujihara (2008) had participants write for 20-minute intervals several times per month. The participants were instructed to write about a traumatic experience, their best possible future selves, or a trivial topic. The researchers found that this simple writing task increased short-term memory capacity after five weeks, but only for the participants who wrote about traumatic experiences."

- **Termeni cheie**: expressive writing, traumatic experience, short-term memory capacity, Yogo and Fujihara

### 6. Self-Reference Effect
Applying the self-reference effect by making information personally meaningful and relating it to one's own life generates a rich network of retrieval cues that aids future recall.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "As you go through the process of elaborative rehearsal, it would be even more beneficial to make the material you are trying to memorize personally meaningful to you. In other words, make use of the self-reference effect."

- **Termeni cheie**: self-reference effect, elaborative rehearsal, personally meaningful, retrieval cues

### 7. Distributed Practice
Distributed practice involves spacing study sessions across time in shorter intervals, which facilitates memory consolidation compared to massed cramming.

> **Citat Verificat Sursă**:
> "Use distributed practice : Study across time in short durations rather than trying to cram it all in at once. Memory consolidation takes time, and studying across time allows time for memories to consolidate."

- **Termeni cheie**: distributed practice, cramming, memory consolidation

## 2. Concepte Extrase și Validate Verbatim

### 1. Cerebellum
The cerebellum is a brain structure essential for the formation of implicit memories, including procedural memory, motor learning, and classical conditioning. Research involving rabbits has demonstrated that damage to the cerebellum prevents the acquisition of conditioned responses, such as the eye-blink reflex.

> "you could still lose it and be able to create implicit memories (procedural memory, motor learning, and classical conditioning), thanks to your cerebellum"

### 2. Prefrontal Cortex
The prefrontal cortex plays a significant role in the processing and retention of information. Studies using PET scans indicate that specific regions of the prefrontal cortex are activated during different types of cognitive tasks, such as semantic versus perceptual processing.

> "From these studies, it seems the prefrontal cortex is involved."

### 3. Semantic Encoding
Semantic tasks, which involve categorizing information based on meaning, lead to better recall compared to perceptual tasks. PET scan data shows that this type of processing is associated with increased activation in the left inferior prefrontal cortex.

> "According to PET scans, there was much more activation in the left inferior prefrontal cortex in the semantic task."

### 4. Frontal Lobe Lateralization
Research suggests a functional division within the frontal regions regarding memory processes. Specifically, the encoding of information is linked to activity in the left frontal region, while the retrieval of information is associated with the right frontal region.

> "In another study, encoding was associated with left frontal activity, while retrieval of information was associated with the right frontal region (Craik et al., 1999)."

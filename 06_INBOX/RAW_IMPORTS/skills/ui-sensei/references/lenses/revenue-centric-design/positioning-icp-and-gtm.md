# RCD Theme: Positioning, ICP & GTM

Part of the `revenue-centric-design` lens — see `revenue-centric-design.md` for the hard gambling-use restriction and attribution requirement before applying anything below.

## Principle: "Who talks to everyone convinces no one"

- **Apply when:** writing landing-page copy, choosing hero messaging, or designing a product's primary value proposition surface.
- **The move:** without a specific ideal customer profile (ICP) in mind, messaging defaults to generic value language that fails to convince anyone in particular — and generic conviction correlates with higher churn even among users who do sign up, because they were never sold on something specific to their situation. Write and design for one named audience, even at the cost of broader appeal.
- **Evidence:** framed by the source as a recurring diagnosis across underperforming landing pages — "no ICP → generic value → churn" is presented as a causal chain, not just a correlation.
- **Visual:** a hero headline naming a specific role/industry/use-case ("Built for solo devs shipping side projects") versus a generic one ("The platform for everyone building software").

## Principle: "Your promise is the size of your proof"

- **Apply when:** deciding how bold a claim to put in hero copy, a case study, or a testimonial section.
- **The move:** the market calibrates belief to demonstrated evidence, not to the size of the claim itself — a bold promise without proof reads as noise, while a modest promise backed by concrete demonstration (a number, a named customer, a before/after) converts better than an unsupported bigger claim.
- **Evidence:** presented as a general pattern in the source's critique of SaaS landing pages that overpromise relative to their visible proof.
- **Visual:** a specific, sourced stat ("Reduced onboarding time by 40% for 200+ teams") next to the claim it supports, rather than an unsupported superlative ("The best onboarding tool ever built").

## Principle: "Same competes on price, different on category"

- **Apply when:** positioning against a crowded set of competitors offering functionally similar features.
- **The move:** if the product reads as functionally the same as competitors, the only lever left is price — a race to the bottom. Differentiate instead on mechanism (how it works), narrative (why it exists), or experience (how it feels to use), so the comparison isn't reducible to a spreadsheet of features and dollars.
- **Evidence:** presented as a strategic framing principle in the source's positioning guidance, not an empirical study — treat it as a heuristic for messaging decisions, not a measured result.
- **Visual:** a comparison page that leads with a distinct mechanism or workflow difference, rather than a feature-parity checklist against competitors.

# RCD Theme: Conversion & Landing Pages

Part of the `revenue-centric-design` lens — see `revenue-centric-design.md` for the hard gambling-use restriction and attribution requirement before applying anything below.

## Principle: neutrality is omission

- **Apply when:** designing any page whose job is to move the visitor toward a specific action (signup, demo request, purchase).
- **The move:** a landing page that presents information neutrally, without directing the visitor toward a next step, is still making a choice — it's choosing not to convert. Every section should have a clear directional intent (what should the visitor do or believe after reading this section), not just informational completeness.
- **Evidence:** framed by the source as a general diagnosis of underperforming landing pages — the ones with the weakest conversion are frequently the most "balanced" or purely informational ones, not the most aggressive ones.
- **Visual:** a features section that ends each block with an implicit or explicit next step (a supporting CTA, a link to a deeper page) rather than a flat list of features with no directional close.

## Principle: gotcha — kill outbound links on the landing page itself

- **Apply when:** building a dedicated conversion-focused landing page (as opposed to a general marketing/content page).
- **The move:** a landing page whose job is to convert one specific action shouldn't offer easy exits to unrelated pages (a full nav menu, blog links, social icons above the fold) — every outbound link is a chance to lose the visitor before they convert. This is scoped specifically to conversion-focused landing pages, not the whole site.
- **Evidence:** flagged explicitly by the source as one of its named "gotchas" — a common mistake teams make by reusing the site-wide nav/footer on a page meant to convert one action.
- **Visual:** a landing page with a minimal or absent top nav, versus the standard site header with a dozen links.

# RCD Theme: Behavioral Science Toolkit

Part of the `revenue-centric-design` lens — see `revenue-centric-design.md` for the hard gambling-use restriction and attribution requirement before applying anything below.

This theme is the general-purpose toolkit of named behavioral mechanisms the other themes draw on — treat it as the reference to pull a specific lever from, rather than a theme tied to one product moment.

## Loss aversion

People weigh a potential loss roughly twice as heavily as an equivalent gain. Applied honestly (see the gotcha in `churn-and-retention.md`), this means framing what a user stands to lose (progress, access, accumulated data) tends to motivate action more than framing an equivalent gain — but only when the loss being described is real, not manufactured.

## Peak-end rule

People judge an experience largely by its most intense moment (peak) and how it ends, not by the average of every moment in between. Applies to onboarding completion screens, checkout confirmations, and offboarding — the last impression disproportionately shapes overall perception of the product, so it's worth deliberate design attention even in a flow that's otherwise functional.

## Eugene Schwartz's awareness levels

A classic copywriting framework (not RCD-original, but foundational to how the source frames messaging): a visitor can be unaware of their problem, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, or fully aware and ready to buy. Messaging pitched at the wrong awareness level underperforms — a fully-aware visitor doesn't need problem education, and an unaware visitor can't process a direct product pitch. Match landing-page copy density and directness to the likely awareness level of the traffic source.

## Decoy effect / asymmetric dominance

See `pricing-and-monetization.md` for the applied version — the general mechanism is that adding a deliberately inferior third option shifts preference toward whichever of the original two options it makes look better by comparison.
